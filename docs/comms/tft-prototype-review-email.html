The idea, in one paragraph

Most AI training gives people a course and stops there. Nobody becomes competent that way; they become briefly informed. The Campus is built on the opposite order — learn a little, then immediately do something real with it, then compare your result with a colleague's. An employee reads a short plain-language lesson, opens a real KOC-shaped problem, builds a model by dragging boxes rather than writing code, submits it, and sees where it placed on a leaderboard. The competition is not a gimmick; it is what turns a lesson somebody attended into a skill somebody practised.

What is in it today

12 learning tracksFrom AI for Everyone — Start Here (no maths, no code, for anyone) through preparing data, predicting a category or a number, forecasting, and judging a model honestly
15 challengesAcross Subsurface, Drilling & Wells, Production, Facilities & Plant, HSE, Maintenance & Reliability, and Medical & Health — the last built around Ahmadi Hospital: screening from a routine lab panel, analyser quality control, emergency triage, and length of stay
Every kind of predictionYes/no, multiple categories, a number, a forecast over time, and finding the abnormal reading nobody labelled
A start with no oil in itThe Titanic challenge — the world's most famous first machine-learning problem — so somebody with no background has an obvious first step
Arabic throughoutThe interface and the learning content both, right-to-left

You do not need any technical background to look at this. If anything on the site requires one, that is a finding I want to hear about.

Please try this path

  1. Register with your name and any email address. It takes a moment.
  2. Open Learn and read the first lesson of AI for Everyone — Start Here. Ask yourself whether a colleague in your directorate with no AI exposure would follow it.
  3. Open Compete and browse the challenges. Look at the one closest to your own area — read the description and the data description, not just the title.
  4. Switch to العربية using the language selector and see whether the Arabic reads naturally to you.
  5. If you have the appetite, build and submit a model on the Titanic challenge and see your score.

What I am asking you for

Please do not send only "it looks good". The specific questions I need answered are:

  1. Is the learning path right? Would somebody in your directorate with no AI background actually get through the first track — and would they want to continue?
  2. Are the challenges credible? Do the problems look like KOC problems to someone who does that work? Which ones are wrong, thin, or missing? What challenge would you add for your own area?
  3. Who is this for first? Which group of colleagues should be the pilot cohort, and why that group?
  4. What would make people actually use it — and what would stop them? Time, permission, relevance, confidence, recognition, something else?
  5. What is missing before this could be taken seriously inside KOC?

Blunt answers are more useful than kind ones. It is far cheaper to change this now than after a rollout.

Two things to be clear about

This is a demonstration host, not a KOC system. It sits with an external hosting provider on the public internet, outside the KOC network and outside Kuwait. Please treat it accordingly: do not upload any real KOC data, and do not use your KOC network password when registering. Every dataset you see on it is synthetic — generated to behave like the real thing — apart from the public Titanic passenger list. A KOC-hosted deployment would sit inside our own environment with corporate sign-in; that is a decision for us to make, not something already done.

It is a prototype, and it will show. You will find rough edges. Note them, but please weigh the idea rather than the finish — whether this way of building capability is right for KOC is the question in front of us.

Where this feeds

Your feedback goes directly into the TFT's three deliverables: the feasibility assessment, the alignment with our organisational goals, and the way forward and action plan. At our next meeting I would like us to reach a view on:
